# Iteration 4: select the simpler learner and test recording mechanisms

The expanded generator's identity learner improves BPI 2012 tune history F1 from 0.8959 to 0.8997 after source-trained participant repair. The source-trained identity control remains better at 0.9107. Running that source control again after refreshing participation reaches 0.9164; a third cycle slightly reduces it to 0.9163. A claim that iterative repair itself is novel or uniquely effective would be unsupported.

On the same 24 expanded generated validation worlds, mean damaged-history F1 is 0.7429 before repair. The boosted edit policy reaches 0.8529, the MLP 0.8243, and the temporal message-passing model 0.8182. Clean-history F1 is 0.99964, 0.99975 and 0.99984 respectively. These are development results selected through generated validation, with a single training seed. They do not support a neural-architecture contribution. Retain the neural systems as controls and carry the simpler learned edit policy forward.

The useful research direction is now an application and benchmark contribution: executable temporal workflows supply repair supervision across interacting participation and identity faults, while an auditable conditional edit learner restores object histories and can abstain. The complete factory is not the paper. Establish real-source transfer, independently implemented recording mechanisms, preservation of genuine exceptions and downstream traceability benefit before asserting publication readiness.

An independent development injector models activity-routed source namespaces, a time-window recording outage, and persistent stale join mappings. These are declared stress mechanisms, not estimated incident frequencies. It shares the canonical anonymizer, not the original per-link fault algorithm. The reference remains evaluator-only; protected event fields are unchanged. No main target was used to design it.

Additional literature checks reinforce the limits on novelty: HoloClean already combines multiple repair signals in probabilistic data cleaning (https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00820), and TIGER separates an observation graph from generated claims for multimodal repair (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00232). Generic iterative correction, evidence separation, or synthetic supervision must not be claimed as first contributions. Our task-specific evidence must concern temporal entity-history repair and its reusable executable benchmark.

Performance work: batched commuting edits replace repeated full-graph traversals. A focused equivalence test checks identical graph/cluster results to sequential application and exact undo. This is an implementation improvement, not a scientific contribution. Original reports retain their source snapshots and timing conditions.

Completed follow-ups: the GraphSAGE adaptation reaches 0.8384 mixed-history F1 and 0.99854 clean-history F1 on the same worlds, below the boosted control. One-pass boosted decoding reaches 0.7996 and fixed-sequence decoding 0.8500. The joint advantage over fixed sequencing is small. Under the independently implemented pipeline faults, the original-fault-trained boosted policy improves only 0.7465 to 0.7811. This is a material robustness weakness, not an established broad transfer result.

The pure synthetic v2 policy, including thresholds selected only on generated validation, improves BPI 2012 tune from 0.744456 to 0.837963. It predicts 18 identity pairs, 17 correct out of 355 true pairs: high precision with very low recall. The source iterative control still reaches 0.9164 and has an additional singleton-Application constraint. Preserve that contract difference in comparisons.

At a fixed training budget of 16 verified source histories, generated-data augmentation improves whole-log development history F1 from 0.835864 to 0.891191. Both models use the same additional source-development calibration sample, so this is not a total 16-history annotation-budget result. Both abstain from identity merging at the chosen precision. Action-level augmentation gains are not uniform: the 256-history ADD control is stronger without augmentation. Full 64/256-history outcomes and repetition remain necessary.

An independent public six-domain simulation collection is now part of the novelty audit and evaluation design. BatteryVault and ClaimStream are development domains; four other scenarios were reserved before record inspection. See EXTERNAL_WORKFLOW_PROTOCOL.md. Their native adapter excludes provenance identifiers, uncorrupted O2O links and relationship qualifiers. The initial available time window was fixed before repair outcomes.

The manuscript in paper/main.tex is an explicitly incomplete development draft. Final confirmation, additional controls, repeated runs and the review package remain outstanding. No acceptance prediction is warranted from the current single-seed development results.
