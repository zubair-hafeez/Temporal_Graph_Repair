      When Generated Workflows Help Temporal Graph Repair:
              Verification Budgets and Merge Safety
                             Anonymous manuscript for author review


                                                 Abstract
          An operational history can lose evidence through missing links, wrong joins or fragmented
      identities. Generated workflows provide exact repair references, but their value must be tested
      against repeated corruption of the same verified real histories and against the errors introduced
      by repair. We compare eight real-history corruption views with the same data plus executable
      generated workflows at matched simulated verification budgets, including fitting and calibration.
      An exploratory study supplies input ablations and fixed models; a prospective confirmation
      evaluates 36 unchanged fits in 768 conditions on 1,024 untouched root histories per source,
      using public loan and purchasing logs with controlled injected faults. At the prespecified 64-
      bundle loan setting with a .99 calibration precision target, generated training raises mean fully
      recovered events from 30.2 to 62.5 per 128-root block and reduces newly damaged events from
      2.08 to 0.00. The paired recovery gain is 32.3 [29.5, 35.3] under conditional 95% block-bootstrap
      intervals, although one of three fitted-seed means loses recovery and mean history F1 falls by
      0.23 percentage points. At 256 bundles, augmentation has worse mean history F1, recovery
      and damage in both sources at both tested targets. Merge guards substantially reduce harm
      relative to unguarded decoding, but a never-merge baseline exposes their residual risk: no tested
      source/training combination gains from merging without added harm. These results establish
      conditional value for generated supervision and quantify the cost of identity recovery. They do
      not establish natural-error performance, human verification savings or automatic-repair safety.


1    Application and contribution
A cancellation can be correctly recorded yet absent from the history retrieved for an order. A
wrong join can make another customer’s record appear in that history. When two systems assign
different identifiers to the same object, either identifier may retrieve only part of its evidence. These
failures make operational investigation diﬀicult even when activities and timestamps themselves are
accurate.
    The practical objective is to restore recorded evidence, including exceptional behavior. We
retain cancellations, returns and repeated work rather than changing the record to resemble an
ideal process. The input is an available window of event–object relations, and the output is an
auditable set of association and identity edits. This is offline reconstruction; later observations
inside the window can support an earlier association.
    Training such a repair system requires examples of correct histories and the ways their observed
connections can fail. Verification is costly because an investigator may need to reconcile several
records and identifiers. Executable workflows offer exact symbolic references, but simulated obser-
vations can contain misleading regularities. For example, an amount may be constant throughout
a simulated order while the corresponding operational measurement changes between activities. A
learner can mistake such differences for incorrect joins.

    We ask two linked application questions. Do generated workflow histories add value beyond
repeated corruption of the same verified operational histories? Do conservative identity merges
recover useful evidence beyond a policy that never merges? Both questions count labels used for
calibration and measure newly introduced errors. Improved average retrieval is insuﬀicient if a
method introduces damaging false associations.
    The contribution is a controlled evaluation protocol for coupled temporal association and iden-
tity repair. It compares generated supervision with repeated corruption of the same verified his-
tories, removes temporal, relational and descriptive evidence before retrieval and refitting, and
tests identity safeguards against complete merge abstention. Untouched histories then test the
exploratory findings with fixed models and thresholds. The classifiers, observation-randomization
principle and graph operators are established components; the contribution is the evidence about
their value and failure conditions under explicit verification and harm accounting.


2     Related work and comparison scope
HERE combines a Transformer with optional expert knowledge for missing case identifiers [1]. We
retain its author model, tensor dataset and greedy decoder in a separate single-case comparison.
Its output contract does not directly express simultaneous multi-object participation edits and alias
merging. SANAGRAPH repairs event-log attributes using supplied trace structure [2]; providing
correct trace membership would expose a target of our task. DDTR and process-tree approaches
recover trace content [3, 4]. Their objectives differ from preserving event content while restoring
its associations.
    Contextual Event Knowledge Graph inference uses available relations and physical rules to
infer missing entity identifiers [8]. Object-centric imperfection patterns identify association and
identity problems encountered in practice [9]. Their public artifacts include injected event–object
faults and observed cross-log object–object disagreements. The latter are useful evidence of data
inconsistency, but do not independently certify the missing or wrong event–object links evaluated
here. We do not claim to introduce these error categories.
    Object-centric simulation [7], configurable process-data faults [10], and temporal graph gener-
ation [14] supply relevant building blocks. Domain randomization is an established transfer strat-
egy [11]; synthetic relational pretraining also has recent precedents [12, 13]. Our question concerns
what these ideas buy an operational repair application under explicit information and verification
limits. HoloClean illustrates joint evidence-based data repair [5]; the present study concentrates
on temporal histories, their identity fragmentation and the consequences of repairing them.


3     Task and executable supervision
3.1   Observed information and permitted edits
An event is e = (u, t, a, P, x): an opaque handle, time, activity, typed participants and legitimate
descriptive fields. Event–object incidence and temporal adjacency within observed histories form
the temporal graph. Equal-time records remain simultaneous; source row order supplies no causal
ordering. A typed catalog contains the available object handles, including any aliases.
    The system can add or remove participation and merge same-type aliases. It cannot invent
events, change recorded activities or times, create entirely unknown entities, or split an already
overmerged identity. A merge consolidates handles and deduplicates participation. These capabil-
ities matter when choosing a dataset: natural errors requiring a different operation cannot validate


                                                 2
this repair vocabulary.
    Only the observed graph and target-type schema enter inference. The canonical identity map,
clean case containers, corruption locations, seeds and clean-derived adjacency remain outside the
learner interface. Target links are corrupted while resources and, where available, vendors remain
context. This is a scoped fault model, not evidence of robustness to unreliable timestamps or
pervasive context corruption. Public logs provide reference representations for controlled fault
injection; they are not certifications that every underlying business record is correct.

3.2   Generate execution, observation and recording faults separately
Executable worlds contain parents, work items, resources and optional batches. Mechanisms include
branching, joins, resource queues, handoffs, multistage routes, repeated work, cancellations and
returns. Queueing configurations enforce resource capacity. The worlds are symbolic workflows,
not calibrated digital twins of the evaluated organizations.
    Observation projection determines visible entity types and fields. Recording faults are applied
afterwards, producing learner observations and separate participation/identity references. Normal-
ized recipes, random states, source fingerprints and export hashes record provenance. Execution
checkpoints reproduce uninterrupted runs. Observations sharing an execution stay in the same
data split.
    The earlier paired observation comparison holds executed events, timestamps, target types
and faults fixed. Generated views retain original fields or introduce activity-dependent categories,
noisy numerical measurements, incidental categories and time bands, with varying field availability.
Measurements can depend on an observed amount and activity, but never on a target identifier.
Clean and damaged versions receive aligned descriptions through builder-only execution ancestry.
Each trial contains 512 fitting worlds and 96 generated calibration worlds. Three independently
generated collections test sensitivity to generated training data.
    The mixed fault family splits eligible histories at a temporal cut with probability .25, removes
approximately .10 of each resulting handle’s links in a contiguous block, and replaces surviving
target links with probability .10 by a different same-type identity. Histories require at least four
events to be fragmented. Missing-block widths use stochastic rounding so short histories can also
lose links. A separately implemented pipeline stressor uses activity-routed aliases, a recording
outage and persistent stale joins. These are declared experimental conditions, not estimates of
natural incident rates.

3.3   Learn and apply auditable edits
Candidate retrieval uses temporal proximity, activity, co-participation and descriptive agreement.
Participation features summarize history degree, temporal bracketing, transitions and contextual
agreement. The query event is excluded from the evidence supporting its existing association.
Identity features compare temporal boundaries and activity, context and field profiles. Opaque
identifier values are not numerical predictors.
    Separate boosted classifiers score additions, removals and merges. An addition is labelled correct
when it supplies a missing reference entity; a removal when the current entity is incorrect; a merge
when both handles refer to the same entity. Earlier generated training includes partial teacher-
repaired states; the repeated-corruption models use initial corrupted states. Local targets do not
guarantee improvement in final history retrieval. Earlier neural controls use the same candidates
with MLP, GraphSAGE [6] or temporal message-passing scorers; architecture superiority is not our
contribution.

4.2   Count fitting and calibration together
A verification unit is a complete root history bundle: the root, its target children, all their alias
handles and event memberships. Total budgets of 16, 64 and 256 bundles allocate 12/48/192 to
fitting and 4/16/64 to calibration. Hash assignments are nested, and a root keeps its role as the
budget increases. Three allocation/fault seeds define the fixed fits. Each role’s graph contains only
its selected histories and their observed context. The two arms receive byte-identical real fitting and
calibration arrays. Table 1 counts their reference access; historical research labels are additional
costs outside these incremental adaptation budgets.
     The R8 arm fits eight damaged views of the same selected histories: mild/severe mixed errors, in-
dependent errors, missing-dominant and wrong-dominant faults, fragmentation and pipeline faults.
Calibration uses three separately seeded mixed, independent and pipeline views of disjoint bundles.
Each view samples at most 256 fitting or 512 calibration event queries, plus retrieved identity pairs.
Query subsampling does not reduce the counted complete-history budget. Artificially corrupted
views use the same underlying reference memberships; they do not represent additional investigator
annotations. Missing positive or negative classes cause operation-specific abstention.
     The +G arm adds a fixed collection of 32 executable worlds, with 512 roots and 4,507 events.
Overlapping arrivals, resource queues, lognormal service times, coarse recording clocks and varying
descriptions include 262 cancellations and 728 retry events. The eight fault variants are applied after
execution. Two additional empty-history graphs admit multiple equally compatible identities and
teach abstention; this is a narrow constructed ambiguity, not independently observed uncertainty.
Generator components are not individually ablated.
     Both arms use 200 boosting iterations, 15 leaves, learning rate .08, regularization 1 and no early
stopping. Augmented fitting initially gives real and generated examples equal weight mass, followed
by class balancing. Thus the arms match real verification, not total examples or compute. For each

Entities with no reference events do not enter Q. Exact-history recovery replaces the inner score

    We separately measure identity-pair precision/recall and changes to initially correct events. The
revised damage measure expands each predicted cluster to all its reference identities before checking
participation. Consequently, a false merge can damage events on both sides, including events whose
representative handle did not change. Direct assignment damage is also retained for comparison
with the earlier study. Identity fragmentation alone can reduce history F1 while canonical event
participation remains correct; a true merge can therefore improve history F1 without increasing
the fully recovered-event count. This is why the never-merge comparison also reports true identity
pairs. Event activity, timestamp and fields are checked to remain unchanged.
    Application queries ask whether a retrieved history contains recorded cancellation evidence and
whether its first/last timestamps are recovered. Cancellation evidence uses the case-insensitive
substring cancel consistently in predictions and references. It does not establish the final business
outcome, and boundary timestamps do not certify every simultaneous boundary record. Earlier
studies report three-seed means and ranges. The current confirmation uses paired block uncertainty
as defined below; events are never treated as independent statistical replications.

4.4   Prospective confirmation and merge abstention
We tested the follow-up’s conditional findings on previously unused target histories, with no addi-
tional training or threshold selection. Before selecting these histories or computing repair outcomes,
a separate protocol fixed all 36 full-information models, both calibration targets, the budget grid,
hypotheses and analysis. The cohort construction above excludes every earlier target-history par-
tition. No new organization, source schema or natural-error annotation is introduced.
    A fixed second hash divides each source into eight disjoint blocks of 128 complete root histories,
matching the exploratory evaluation size. All records of each selected root are retained, and each
block is repaired separately. Inference context therefore remains 128 roots; full-log scaling is outside
this experiment. Each block receives three new mixed-fault realizations, paired with the three
previously fitted model seeds. Every budget and training arm sees the identical damaged graph
within a block/seed. The complete experiment has 576 guarded budget/threshold conditions, plus
192 safety conditions at budget 64 and q = .99. No-edit outcomes are retained for all 48 block/seed
inputs. No additional generated examples or verified fitting/calibration histories are supplied.
    All confirmation policies use one simultaneous edit pass with atomic root substitutions. The
safety comparison reuses identical candidates, scores and calibrated thresholds. Guarded decoding
rejects tied additions and permits a merge only at score at least .99, without co-occurring handles,

                                                    6
and when it is mutual best with a margin of at least .05 over alternatives. The never-merge
policy prohibits all alias merges while preserving the guarded policy’s tied-addition rejection and
atomic root substitutions. It can still repair participation and can still introduce wrong links. An
unguarded policy retains the atomic-root contract but removes the additional confidence, margin
and co-occurrence checks. Thus the comparison tests whether conservative merging adds value
beyond complete merge abstention.
    Two directional hypotheses were fixed. H1 predicts that augmentation at 64 bundles and q = .99
increases loan full-event recovery without increasing mean new damage. H2 predicts that guarded
merging retains true identity pairs and improves history F1 or recovery over never merge without
increasing mean new damage, tested separately for each source/arm. For uncertainty, we first
average the three fixed model/fault runs within each block, then bootstrap eight paired blocks
with 10,000 resamples. We report conditional 95% percentile intervals. They describe new-history
variation for these fixed models in these processes; they do not treat events or seeds as independent
populations. Interval support requires a positive lower bound for the gain and a nonpositive upper
bound for damage. Secondary comparisons have no multiplicity adjustment.

4.5   Supporting published-method comparison
The native comparison independently masks each event’s single root identifier with probability .30
in each 2,048-root cohort, using three fixed seeds. Activity, resource and time are available; child
identifiers, vendor relations and descriptive fields are removed for every method. Trusted remaining
case IDs provide closed-case supervision. HERE’s author Transformer uses 512-dimensional states,
three layers, eight heads, 15-event windows, batch size eight and 30 epochs. The final epoch
is evaluated. Only retained identifiers enter training targets; overlapping-window validation is
diagnostic, not an independent test. The headless runner and masking/evaluation protocol are
explicit adaptations. No expert rules are supplied, so the comparison is named HERE-Transformer
without expert rules, not a reproduction of the complete published system.
    Matched controls include both fixed generated scorers, a target-local boosted scorer and nearest-
known-event matching. Generated scorers choose one candidate for each missing ID. The local
boosted control fits at most 4,096 retained-ID queries with leave-query-out profiles, whereas HERE
uses the remaining known events throughout its training windows; this unequal fitting volume is
reported. All retained memberships are observed context. Existing IDs remain unchanged. Native
results therefore assess missing-ID completion, not robustness to wrong existing IDs, coupled alias
repair or verification-budget eﬀiciency. Data, fixed generated weights, implementation and all
six native conditions were pinned before native outcomes; newly fitted checkpoints were recorded
afterwards. The native comparison finished before the separate 156-job combined-repair protocol
was frozen. That declaration retained both generated mixtures and all budget comparisons, with
no subsequent model or threshold selection from confirmation outcomes.

5.2   Exploratory repeated-corruption study
The precursor study fitted 60 models and evaluated 156 repair conditions, including the full-input
models reused for confirmation and the input ablations below. Choices were frozen before the
new model outcomes. Evaluation uses 128 hash-selected roots from each previously evaluated
confirmation cohort: 4,942 loan and 2,792 purchasing events. These reused source histories make
this an exploratory follow-up, not independent confirmation. A single simultaneous proposal pass
commits root replacements atomically. Both fixed calibration targets, .95 and .99, are reported.
A guard rejects tied additions and permits merges only at score at least .99, without co-occurring
handles, and with mutual-best scores separated from alternatives by .05. The same guard applies to
both training arms. Its observed harm must still be measured; score thresholds are not guarantees.
Different cohort size, context and decoding make these scores non-comparable to the earlier four-
pass results.

5.3   Earlier evidence and comparator sensitivity
The earlier paired observation study held executions and faults fixed while varying visible generated
fields. Varied supervision raised mean history F1 from .791 to .805 on loans and from .809 to .826
on purchasing; a separate 96-world generated confirmation improved from .759 to .800. Limits were
also visible: the loan pipeline condition decreased F1 in all three trials, and real-source training
outperformed generated transfer on the BPI 2012 check. The full paired table and failures remain
in the artifact.
    The earlier combined-repair experiment evaluated 156 declared conditions on 1,024-root cohorts,
using one real corruption view and four decoding passes. On purchasing, the varied mixture with
64 verified bundles reached 85.6% history F1 versus 80.6% for real-only training, winning all three
paired trials; it also exceeded the 84.5% real-only mean at 256 bundles. It raised exact-history
recovery from 36.5% to 48.7% and fully recovered 3,880 initially incorrect events versus 2,992,
while damaging 237 versus 411 previously correct events. These are conditional gains against that
single-corruption baseline, not evidence of a fourfold reduction in human verification time. The
new repeated-corruption comparisons in Tables 2 and 5 directly address the stronger comparator.
    Earlier application queries also showed why history F1 alone is insuﬀicient. Purchasing
cancellation-evidence F1 increased from 89.3% to 91.1%, but on loans improved cancellation
retrieval coexisted with reduced first-timestamp accuracy. One calibration-only 16-bundle loan
run damaged 9,028 initially correct events. Tightening calibration from .95 to .99 reduced damage
and recovery in all four tested source/regime combinations. Every earlier method introduced
some damage across its clean-input trials, and generated mixtures could increase damage under
pipeline faults. Full earlier budget, operational-query, threshold and stress tables remain available
with their original protocols; their different context, generator and decoder prevent direct score
comparisons with the current confirmation.

6   Scope, limitations and reproducibility
The earlier paired study supports observation variation as a useful generated-data condition. Its
purchasing budget advantage is relative to a single-corruption real-only control. The fresh repeated-
corruption confirmation supports the prespecified strict-loan recovery/harm benefit, while rejecting
general augmentation superiority and a cost-free merge-safety benefit. These studies are not gen-
eral sample-complexity results, measured reductions in human work, or evidence of transfer to an
unseen institution. The repeated-corruption models use a different bounded generated collection,
selected-history context and one-pass decoding from the earlier study; differences between studies
cannot be attributed solely to corruption count. Fixed mixture weighting and individual generator
mechanisms were not separately optimized or ablated.
    All operational error labels in the main evaluation come from controlled perturbations of re-
leased representations. Public natural-error candidates were audited without substituting their
tasks: S2AND identity disagreements largely require splitting overmerged profiles, while the public
object-centric imperfection demonstration compares object–object relationships across logs. Nei-
ther provides independent natural business-error ground truth compatible with the implemented
operations. Field validation with verified operational corrections remains open.
    Sparse or empty histories may not contain enough information to identify a missing participant.
False merges can contaminate many retrieved histories. Confidence thresholds fitted on a few
bundles are uncertain, and precision measured on those bundles is not a deployment guarantee.
The fault families, uncorrupted context, available catalog and offline window all limit transfer
claims. Sampling complete roots also changes concurrency relative to a complete enterprise log.
    The original study included a BPI 2012 check [15], architecture controls and generated compo-
sitional tests. Their results and failures remain in the separate supporting artifact. Four originally
declared external simulation sources could not be retrieved; their planned evaluations remain ex-
plicitly unexecuted. No substitute was chosen based on outcomes. The revision preserves that

    Recipes, split rules, label-access tests, source/model hashes, predictions, edit audits and per-trial
results support reproduction. The earlier 156 combined-repair outputs passed saved-edit replay,
event-content preservation and independent metric checks. The native audit reconstructs truth from
pinned source memberships. Both domains’ original revision partitions were rebuilt byte-identically.
Refitting six earlier budget-64 models reproduced calibration decisions and thresholds, with score
differences below 1.4×10−13 ; model-file byte identity did not reproduce. The exploratory and fresh-
history studies retain separate frozen protocols, per-trial outcomes and resource measurements. The
fresh-history study reuses existing fits: its uncertainty analysis is conditional on those models and
the two known source processes. Stored-model replay is distinct from fresh retraining; no human
annotation-time study or independent hardware replication is claimed.


7    Conclusion
Untouched-history confirmation supports one prespecified benefit of generated supervision beyond
repeated corruption of verified histories: greater loan event recovery with less observed harm at
a strict, limited-budget operating point. At 256 bundles, the fixed augmentation has worse mean
history F1, recovery and damage in both sources at both targets. It also loses on the main purchas-
ing comparisons. Conservative identity checks substantially reduce damage relative to unguarded
merging, but never merge exposes a remaining tradeoff between complete histories and new errors.
A strong repair evaluation therefore needs a competitive corruption baseline, a merge-abstention
control, and separate recovery and harm endpoints. Natural-error validation, new institutions and
measured verification effort remain necessary to establish field value.
